## COMPLETE CPU SPECS: 1990–2026

- **Transistors**: 3.1–3.3M | **Process**: 800–600 nm | **Clock**: 60–200 MHz | **Cache**: 8KB L1i + 8KB L1d

- **Transistors**: 7.5–19M | **Process**: 350–250 nm | **Clock**: 233–500 MHz | **Cache**: 512 KB L2
- **Die**: ~113 mm² (Slot 1) | **IPC**: 1.5 | **MSRP**: $200–$500

### Pentium III/Pentium 4 (2000–2005)
- **Pentium III Coppermine**: 28M transistors, 700 MHz–1.4 GHz, 256 KB L2 on-die, SSE introduced
- **Pentium 4 Willamette**: 42M transistors, 1.4–2.0 GHz, 20-stage pipeline, 256 KB L2, Hyperthreading
- **Pentium 4 Prescott (90 nm)**: 125M transistors, 2.8–3.8 GHz, 31-stage pipeline, **10 W per GHz**, 1 MB L2
- **Note**: P4 was an **architectural misstep** — deep pipeline, high frequency, poor IPC. Intel's share dropped 10–15% in enthusiast market. **Design lesson**: a player pursuing "deep pipeline, high frequency" naively recreates the P4 curve.

### Athlon XP (2001–2004)
- **Barton (130 nm)**: 54.3M transistors, 1.6–2.2 GHz, 256 KB L2, **~2.0 IPC**, 68 W (2.2 GHz)

### Pentium M / Core Duo / Core 2 (2003–2007)
- **Pentium M**: 77M transistors, 130 nm, 1.3–2.3 GHz, 2 MB L2, **ultra-low power (~5–27 W)**, mobile-focused
- **Core Duo**: 151M transistors, 65 nm, 1.6–2.3 GHz, 2–4 MB L2 shared, 31–44 W TDP
- **Core 2 Duo**: 291M transistors, 65 nm, 1.6–2.93 GHz, 4 MB L2, **dominated market 2006–2010**
- **Inflection**: Core 2 was Intel's triumphant comeback post-P4. x86-64 (introduced AMD64 2003, Intel adopted 2004) was **mandatory** by 2006.

### Core i3/i5/i7 (2008–2026)
- **1st gen Nehalem (2008, 45 nm)**: i7-920 (quad, 2.66 GHz), 731M transistors, 8 MB L3, Hyper-Threading, QPI, 130 W, MSRP $999
- **Sandy Bridge (2nd gen, 32 nm, 2011)**: i7-2600K (quad, 3.4 GHz base), 995M transistors, Turbo Boost 2.0

### EUV Lithography

**Wavelength**: 13.5 nm (vs. 193 nm ArF immersion).

**Adoption**: TSMC N7 (2018 pilot), N5 (2020 volume). **ASML EUV scanner** $120–$200M each, only 1–2 shipped/quarter (2018–2021), now 3–5/quarter.

**Throughput**: EUV 60–80 wafers/hour (goal 150 wph by 2027). ArF ~120–150 wph. **EUV is bottleneck** — limits fab capacity.

**Mask cost**: EUV $100M–$300M per design (vs. $5M–$15M ArF). **First-wafer cost is extreme**, non-viable for low-volume.

**Game mechanic**: EUV is **high-risk, high-reward**.
- Build EUV fab: $28–48B (vs. $9–20B ArF).
- Player needs $28B+ cash + bank credit.
- 24–36 month construction, 6–12 month ramp to 50% yield.
- Only then does production occur.

**AI behavior**: Rivals commit to EUV 18–24 months before first wafer. Competitors anticipate advantage, design for future nodes. Model as **procurement commitment event**: announce "EUV by 2025", if delayed rivals prepare alternates.

### Yield Modeling (Poisson)

**Yield formula**: Y = exp(−D₀ × A)
- **D₀** = defect density (defects/cm²)
- **A** = die area (cm²)

**Example**: D₀ = 0.1 def/cm², die = 0.5 cm² → Y = exp(−0.05) = 95.1% good dies.

| Node | Mature D₀ | Ramp-phase D₀ (yr 1) | Improvement/yr |
|------|-----------|----------------------|-----------------|
| 130 nm | 0.02 | 0.08 | –30%/yr |
| 90 nm | 0.04 | 0.15 | –30%/yr |
| 45 nm | 0.08 | 0.35 | –35%/yr |
| 22 nm | 0.15 | 0.60 | –40%/yr |
| 7 nm | 0.40 | 1.20 | –45%/yr |
| 5 nm | 0.60 | 1.80 | –50%/yr |
| 3 nm | 1.00+ | 2.50+ | –55%/yr |

**Game implication**: New nodes **start hot** (1.2–2.5× mature D₀), need 18–36 months ramp. Launching flagship on brand-new node risks:
- Yr 1: 60–70% yield → low volume, high per-unit cost.
- Yr 2: 75–80% yield → achievable volumes.
- Yr 3: 85–90% yield → competitive.

**Cost penalty**: 70% yield chip costs 1.43× more per good die than 85%. If BOM $200, margin ($299 − $200 = $99) becomes barely-breakeven.

### Die Area & Wafer Yield

**300 mm wafer**: ~70,690 mm² usable area. 

**Formula**: good_dies_per_wafer = floor(70690 / die_area_mm²) × Y × 0.92 (edge loss)

**Example**: 100 mm² die, 85% yield → floor(706.9) × 0.85 × 0.92 ≈ **621 good dies/wafer**.

### Fab Capacity & Economics

**Modern 300 mm fab**: ~100,000 wafers/month full utilization (multi-shift). **GigaFab** = 1M WPM, ~$100B equipment base estimate.

**TSMC (2024)**: ~15 fabs, ~3.0M WPM total. Advanced (5nm–): ~600k WPM fully allocated 2024–25. Mature (28nm+): ~1.5M WPM, ~70% utilization, absorbs +200k WPM.

**Game cap**: Realistic fab ownership. Single 300 mm fab = 100k WPM. Real companies own 3–15 fabs over decades.

## MARKET SIMULATION: OLIGOPOLY, PRICING, ADOPTION CURVES

### Bertrand Competition (Price-setting)

In Bertrand model, firms compete on price. With differentiated products (IPC, power, brand):

```
Price_floor = MC + (brand_premium × brand_value)
MC ≈ BOM + mfg_overhead + licensing
```

**Real example (RTX 4090, Feb 2023)**:
- BOM: ~$500–600 (VRAM $180, GPU die $150, substrate $50, interconnect $30, PCB $40, misc $80)
- Mfg overhead: ~$100 (amortized fab, QA, logistics)
- MC ≈ $600–700
- MSRP: $1,599
- **Brand premium**: $1,599 − $700 = $899 (NVIDIA's software ecosystem, driver support, DLSS dominance)

**Game formula**:
```
MSRP = MC × (1 + margin%) + brand_premium
margin% = base_margin × (perf_rating / competitor_avg) × demand_factor
brand_premium = brand_value × (0.1 + AI_personality.marketing)
```

### Logistic (S-Curve) Adoption

New technology (e.g., 64-bit x86, Hyper-Threading, 5G):
```
adoption(t) = max_adoption / (1 + exp(-k × (t - t_inflection)))
```

**Historical curves**:
- **x86-64**: Intro 2003, 50% adoption 2008, 95% by 2015.
- **Hyper-Threading**: Intro 2002, 50% adoption 2006, 90% by 2012.
- **5G modem**: Intro 2019, 50% adoption 2021, 80% by 2025.

**Game**: Each new tech unlock auto-adds adoption curve. Ship too early (<30% max adoption) = lukewarm response. Too late (>90% saturation) = competitive advantage gone.
